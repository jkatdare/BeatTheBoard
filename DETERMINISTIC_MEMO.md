WHERE PURE LOGIC BREAKS\n\nSpecific situations where multiple tracks are equally feasible and a human picks:\n\n- **The midday turn with an empty board.** A 6-car NEC turn at 13:40. C1 leaves {1..19}, C9 prefers {1..16}, no length exclusion, two neighbors posted. Survivor set ~10. Nothing in physics distinguishes track 3 from track 8. The dispatcher picks by crew position, cleaning crew location, or habit.\n- **Same-clique symmetry.** Two identical NJT turns, same length, same direction, overlapping windows, three free tracks. The constraint model is *symmetric* — swapping them is equally feasible. Solvers famously exploit this symmetry; dispatchers break it by convention you can't see.\n- **Route-set-on-assumption.** FRA documents that departure readiness runs on \"several informal systems,\" including dispatchers who \"assume a train will be ready to depart at the scheduled departure time and route track switches and signals for the movement.\" That's why your berth coordinate can populate early **and then change**. The early signal is a *guess by the dispatcher*, not a commitment.\n- **Disruption cascades.** Platform fire, a signal problem, a lost door light, a medical aid, an East River tunnel out for weekend work — a crew member reports loading NJT on **track 19** during a platform fire. All bands dissolve. Everything the model knows about \"which track this train usually gets\" lives in that table, and **the table is an input, not a deduction.** NJT publishes no equivalent. You have to mine it from history — which is already a statistical step. There is no constraint that encodes \"the 5:47 to Trenton goes from 4 because the crew room is at the west end.\"\n\n**Two independent results confirm optimizers ≠ dispatchers:**\n- Caprara et al. beat RFI's incumbent operational tool by **40–99%** on the objective. RECOMMENDED ARCHITECTURE — hybrid, and here's the honest minimum\n\n**What elimination buys:** 21 → 5–9 typical, 2–3 in favorable cases, exactly 1 in a ~3–8% minority. Plus it makes every ranking downstream dramatically cheaper and more accurate, because the model only has to discriminate among survivors instead of 16 classes. It also gives you something no competitor offers: **defensible negative information** (\"not tracks 1–6, this is a 12-car set\") that is *provably* correct and can be shown at T-20 min, long before any track posts.\n\n**Can you remove the data science entirely?**\n\n**No — but you can remove the *machine learning*, and that's the useful distinction.** The minimum statistical layer is not a model. It is one `GROUP BY`:\n\n```sql\nSELECT line, movement_type, consist_len_bucket, dow_type, hour, track, COUNT(*)\nFROM observed_departures            -- from your own board scrape + GPS decode\nGROUP BY 1,2,3,4,5,6\n```\n\nThat table is an empirical reconstruction of Caprara's `L_{d₁,d₂}` preference list — the same object that RFI's *real, deployed optimizer* takes as an **input** because it can't be derived either. You need roughly 4–8 weeks of scraped history to populate it. No gradient descent, no features, no training loop, no ML dependency. A frequency table with Laplace smoothing, renormalized over the surviving candidate set, will outperform nypenn.live's 46% top-1.\n\nIf someone insists \"no statistics at all,\" the fallback is a **pure ordering heuristic**: rank survivors by A-Interlocking crossing count ascending (RPA's table: track 11 crosses 0, track 1 crosses 7), tiebreak by track number. This is fully deterministic and will be meaningfully worse than the frequency table, because it optimizes what a *good* dispatcher would do rather than what *this* dispatcher does — the exact gap Caprara and Sels both measured.\n\n**Direction of the split matters.** The mature analogue is airport gate assignment, which runs ML on the *uncertain continuous inputs* (arrival times) and combinatorics on the *discrete assignment*. **You want the mirror image: combinatorics to prune, counting to choose.** Don't build a 16-class classifier over raw features; you'll relearn the physical constraints badly from data when you can just assert them.\n\n**High-value ingest to add:** the LIRR GTFS-RT feed (keyless, `.../lirr%2Fgtfs-lirr`, field 1005). Be clear-eyed about what it buys — post-flip LIRR arrivals land on **14/15/16**, departures on **14/17/18**, and only at T=0. It removes at most ~3 tracks from NJT's feasible set, with no advance warning. Worth a day of work, not a week.\n\n**Ship the candidate set, not a point estimate.** Every deployed comparable exposes provenance and confidence (`platsrc`/`conf`, RTT's grey-vs-black, nypenn.live's color tiers). Given that a real MILP called the real operating schedule infeasible at 4 of 10 stations, a hard deterministic assertion will be wrong in ways your users cannot diagnose.
